\hypertarget{lock-free-programming}{%
\section{LOCK-FREE PROGRAMMING}\label{lock-free-programming}}

Welcome to the most dangerous and rewarding part of C++. Lock-free
programming is like performing open-heart surgery while the patient is
running a marathon.

\hypertarget{the-atomic-coffee-shop-analogy}{%
\subsubsection{The Atomic Coffee Shop
Analogy}\label{the-atomic-coffee-shop-analogy}}

Imagine a busy coffee shop with many customers (threads) and one barista
(the data).

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Mutex (The Locked Door)}: To talk to the barista, you have to
  lock the front door of the shop. No one else can even enter until you
  are done. This is safe, but if you take 10 minutes to order, theres a
  giant line outside.
\item
  \textbf{Lock-Free (The Ticket System)}: Everyone is in the shop at
  once. The barista has a ``Current Ticket'' number. You look at your
  ticket, and if it matches the current number, you swap it for your
  coffee in one instant motion. If someone else gets there first, your
  ticket is ``out of date,'' and you have to go to the back of the line
  and try again.
\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{why-do-we-care}{%
\paragraph{Why do we care?}\label{why-do-we-care}}

In high-frequency trading (HFT), waiting for a Mutex is like waiting for
a slow elevator. Lock-free code is like a high-speed conveyor belt.

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{the-voucher-exchange-compare-and-swap}{%
\subsubsection{The ``Voucher Exchange''
(Compare-And-Swap)}\label{the-voucher-exchange-compare-and-swap}}

The heart of lock-free is \textbf{CAS (Compare-And-Swap)}. Think of it
as an ``Honest Exchange'':

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  You show the Barista a photo of the counter as it looked 10 seconds
  ago (\textbf{Old Value}).
\item
  You say: ``If the counter still looks exactly like this photo, put
  this coffee on it (\textbf{New Value}).''
\item
  The Barista looks. If it matches, the swap happens instantly. If it
  \emph{doesn't} match (someone else moved a cup), the Barista says
  ``Transaction Denied,'' and hands you a \emph{new} photo of the
  counter.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{the-aba-problem-the-water-cooler-analogy}{%
\subsubsection{The ABA Problem: The Water Cooler
Analogy}\label{the-aba-problem-the-water-cooler-analogy}}

The biggest trap in lock-free is the \textbf{ABA Problem}.

Imagine you see a full bottle on the water cooler (Value A). You leave
to get a cup. While you are gone: 1. Friend 1 drinks all the water
(Value B). 2. Friend 2 refills the bottle with swamp water (Value A
again).

You come back, see the bottle is ``full'' (Value A), and drink it. You
think nothing changed, but everything changed!

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Godhood Tip}: To solve this, we use ``Tagged Pointers'' or
``Hazard Pointers'' to track not just the value, but \emph{how many
times} it has changed.
\end{quote}

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{the-concept}{%
\subsection{1. The Concept}\label{the-concept}}

Programming without Mutexes. Guarantees system-wide progress.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Lock-Free:} At least one thread always makes progress.
\item
  \textbf{Wait-Free:} Every thread makes progress in finite steps.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{compare-and-swap-cas}{%
\subsection{2. Compare-And-Swap (CAS)}\label{compare-and-swap-cas}}

The primitive of lock-free.
\passthrough{\lstinline!compare\_exchange\_weak!} vs
\passthrough{\lstinline!compare\_exchange\_strong!}.

\begin{lstlisting}[language={C++}]
std::atomic<int> head;

void push(int new_val) {
    int old_head = head.load();
    // Loop until we successfully swap head with new_val
    while (!head.compare_exchange_weak(old_head, new_val)) {
        // old_head is updated to current head value automatically
    }
}
\end{lstlisting}

\hypertarget{the-aba-problem}{%
\subsection{3. The ABA Problem}\label{the-aba-problem}}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Thread 1 reads A.
\item
  Thread 2 changes A to B, then back to A.
\item
  Thread 1 CAS(A, new) succeeds, thinking nothing changed.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Solutions:} * \textbf{Versioned Pointers:} Store
\passthrough{\lstinline!\{ptr, count\}!}.
\passthrough{\lstinline!std::atomic<uint128\_t>!} (if supported). *
\textbf{Hazard Pointers:} Protect pointers currently being read. *
\textbf{RCU (Read-Copy-Update):} Wait for all readers to finish before
reclaiming memory.

\hypertarget{lock-free-data-structures}{%
\subsection{4. Lock-Free Data
Structures}\label{lock-free-data-structures}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Lock-Free Stack:} Easy (CAS on head).
\item
  \textbf{Lock-Free Queue:} Harder (Head and Tail). Use Michael-Scott
  Queue algorithm.
\item
  \textbf{Lock-Free Hash Map:} Very hard (Split-Ordered Lists).
\end{itemize}
